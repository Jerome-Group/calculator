Coverage & reference

Calculator combines symbolic mathematics, numerical methods and graphs in one local workspace. The operation finder currently contains 128 operations, with editable parameters and examples. This page describes the implemented scope and its limits.

Coverage reviewed against the current operation catalogue, 27 September 2026. A supported field means useful computations on its objects; it does not mean every problem in that field has an algorithmic solution.

Your workspace

Sign in with ChatGPT to open your own notebooks. Saves are scoped to your account, with a device copy for offline work. Use Settings to import earlier device notebooks explicitly; the original copy is kept. Concurrent changes preserve both notebook versions after reload. Computation stays in your browser.

Offline preparation verifies every required asset and saves the signed-in app shell. It can resume after interruption. Stay signed in on this device; sign-out removes offline reopening. Browser storage eviction still requires online preparation again.

Graphs and reusable work

Plot multiple Cartesian functions, implicit relations, inequalities, parametric/polar curves, sequences, direction fields and 3D surface wireframes. Pan, zoom, fit, trace and inspect tables. Cartesian analysis includes values, roots, intersections, critical points, tangents, integrals, areas and domain checks. Derivative/integral views produce additional graphs.

Save functions, matrices, datasets and expressions by name. Notebooks keep names separate. History records inputs, settings and definition snapshots; changing a definition does not rewrite old answers. Use Use, Save as or a structured result’s Save value to continue a calculation.

Graph rendering is numerical sampling. Narrow features can be missed; steep segments may be left open. Implicit relations use a finite grid, and surface views are wireframes. The graph expression evaluator supports a smaller function vocabulary than the symbolic engine; an exact special-function answer is not guaranteed to have a directly renderable graph.

Read results with their conditions

  • Exact means the returned symbolic/exact representation, subject to the displayed domain, assumptions and conditions. It does not certify a universal theorem or all exceptional parameter cases.
  • Approximate results from numerical algorithms generally use machine floating-point arithmetic. The precision setting controls supported symbolic numerical evaluation from 5 to 200 decimal digits; increasing it does not increase every numerical solver’s precision.
  • Unresolved means some computation remains conditional or unevaluated. An engine failure or timeout does not establish that a solution or closed form does not exist. An empty solution set has a different meaning.
  • Numerical roots and optimisation results apply to the stated bracket, initial guess and constraints. They do not establish that every root or the global optimum was found. Error estimates and residuals are diagnostics, not universal error proofs.
  • Complex elementary functions use the engine’s principal branches. Symbolic matrices can change rank at exceptional parameter values. Review the operation’s restrictions before substituting such values.
  • For discrete probabilities, CDF means P(X ≤ x), while survival means P(X > x). An inclusive upper tail P(X ≥ k) uses survival at k−1. Statistics identify sample/population denominators, confidence methods and test tails.

Finite computational scope

Object or methodBound or limitation
MatricesOperation inputs up to 30 × 30; the touch grid editor supports up to 12 × 12. Symbolic expression size may become expensive well before those limits.
Finite groupsFull enumeration up to order 256 and permutation degree 16, with at most 16 generators. Permutations use zero-based image arrays. The multiplication convention is shown in the result; quotient groups require normal subgroups.
Finite quotientsPrime coefficient modulus; polynomial degree at most 12 and quotient size at most 65,536. A field requires an irreducible modulus. A reducible quotient is a ring and can have nonzero elements without inverses.
Topology & relationsRational homology: at most 64 vertices, 512 generated nonempty simplices, simplex size 7, and a boundary-work limit. It does not compute integral torsion. Finite topologies: at most 10 points and the full open-set family. Relations: at most 64 points. No general homeomorphism or fundamental-group solver.
Logic & graphsTruth tables up to 10 variables. Graph analysis up to 200 nodes and 1,000 edges. Directed cycle enumeration is restricted to small graphs.
PDEs & geometryHeat/wave tools accept up to 50 sine modes with zero endpoint values; omitted modes are not inferred. Poisson uses a 3–35 point interior grid per side with zero boundary values. Geometric charts must be provably real and regular. These are specific problem families, not unrestricted PDE or manifold solvers.

Use Cancel for expensive work. General nonlinear solving, symbolic integration, convergence, multivariable limits and parameter-dependent algebra can remain unresolved even for short inputs.

Prepare for offline use

Calculation runs locally. Sign in with ChatGPT to open your personal workspace. After loading, calculation uses local engines and needs no calculation server, API key or runtime AI service. Account saves sync when connected. Reopening offline is a separate browser capability.

  1. Open Settings & saved work → Offline preparation → Prepare for offline use while connected.
  2. Keep the tab open until preparation reports Ready. Preparation verifies the versioned application, engines, packages, workers and fonts, including assets for capabilities you have not opened.
  3. If interrupted, reconnect and select Verify / repair offline files or retry preparation. Previously saved files are checked again.
  4. For offline reopening, use the same browser/profile and the same published address. It requires a secure context and service-worker/storage support. Browser eviction, private browsing, cleared data and host sign-in requirements can prevent reopening.

Verification scope: the production build completed preparation after an intentionally interrupted package download. With its local origin disconnected, the app reopened, preserved work, computed a previously unopened histogram and ODE, and rendered a new surface and trajectories. This verifies that tested browser, not every browser or future hosting policy. Test reopening on your own device before relying on it.

Back up and recover

Choose Settings & saved work → Export backup to download a JSON backup containing notebooks, definitions, history and graphs. Store it somewhere independent of this browser. Import backup validates the file and adds separate notebook copies. Work is saved separately for each signed-in account and synced between devices. A local copy supports offline work. Conflicting revisions preserve separate notebook copies on reload. Legacy device-only work can be imported explicitly in Settings; its original remains available.

If the latest save cannot be read, Calculator attempts the previous valid save. The settings panel also offers Restore previous valid save; unreadable stored data can be exported for recovery when automatic saving is paused. Browser storage is useful, but it is not a permanent backup.

Foundations and sources

Computation is built on bundled Pyodide 0.27.7, SymPy 1.13.3, SciPy 1.14.1, NumPy 2.0.2 and NetworkX 3.4.2. MathLive 0.110.0 supports mathematical entry; KaTeX 0.16.22 renders results; mathjs 15.2.0 evaluates graph expressions with Calculator's legacy percentage grammar preserved. Dependency licences ship with the application.

  • Scope informed by the MIT mathematics catalogue and NUS mathematics and interdisciplinary programmes, with the TI-84 Plus CE capability list as a practical reference. These sources inform coverage; they are not endorsements or a claim of course/exam compatibility.
  • Symbolic result semantics: SymPy solution sets, matrices, PDE method scope and differential geometry.
  • Finite algebra: permutation groups and polynomial coefficient domains. Prime-power extension fields here use polynomial quotients; integers modulo a composite number are not treated as a field.
  • Numerical foundations: SciPy statistics, optimisation and NetworkX algorithms.
  • Computational topology reference: Sage finite simplicial complexes. Calculator uses exact boundary-matrix linear algebra for its bounded rational homology operation; Sage itself is not bundled.

Design and repair notes

Structured matrix, system, integral, logic and distribution forms show the problem before calculation. Results label assignments, bases, multiplicities, factors, convergence conditions and truth values. The unit-circle contour uses the exact residue theorem; general finite complex integrals receive numerical cross-checks, with disagreements explicitly approximate.

New explicit tools include least squares, numerical SVD, Cholesky, metric curvature, independent uncertainty propagation, finite expectation, symbolic random variables, histograms and box plots.

Design references: Jerome dashboard, Linear, Instrument / ŌURA, Pentagram / IBM, Work & Co / VistaPrint. These informed compact navigation, progressive detail and a consistent mathematical visual vocabulary.
